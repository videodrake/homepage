Dietary Supplementation of Octacosanol Improves Exercise-Induced
Fatigue and Its Molecular Mechanism

ABSTRACT: Several publications report that octacosanol (OCT) has diﬀerent biological functions. This study was designed to
evaluate the antifatigue eﬀect and molecular mechanism of octacosanol (200 mg/(kg day)) in forced exercise-induced fatigue models
of trained male C57BL/6 mice. Results showed that octacosanol ameliorated the mice’s autonomic activities, forelimb grip strength,
and swimming endurance, and the levels of liver glycogen (LG), muscle glycogen (MG), blood lactic acid (BLA), lactate
dehydrogenase (LDH), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) were also regulated. Gene analysis
results showed that treatment with OCT upregulated 29 genes, while 38 genes were downregulated in gastrocnemius tissue. Gene
ontology (GO) analyses indicated that these genes enriched functions in relation to myoﬁbril, contractile ﬁber, and calcium-
dependent adenosinetriphosphatase (ATPase) activity. Octacosanol supplementation signiﬁcantly adjusted the messenger RNA
(mRNA) and protein expression levels related to fatigue performance. Octacosanol has an observably mitigating eﬀect in exercise-
induced fatigue models, and its molecular mechanism may be related to the regulation of tripartite motif-containing 63 (Trim63),
periaxin (Prx), calcium voltage-gated channel subunit α1 H (Cacna1h), and myosin-binding protein C (Mybpc3) expression.
KEYWORDS: antifatigue, octacosanol, energy and endurance performance, biochemical parameters, microarray, molecular mechanism
■INTRODUCTION
Fatigue is one of the most common physiological reactions
related to a progressive gradual decline in the ability for vitality
and activity.1 Fatigue can either be physiologic or chronic.
Physiological fatigue is the disability of muscles to maintain
normal movements due to severe stress and intensive physical
or mental activity.2 Therefore, exercise fatigue is a distinguish-
ingly physiological process after strenuous exercise that
involves a signiﬁcant decrease in the ability for more work,
vitality, and activities.3 This situation mainly results from the
depletion of energy sources and the accumulation of end
products that eventually lead to the disorder in the internal
environment of the body.4 Asides from physiological reaction
to physical stress, fatigue is also indicative of disorders, such as
immunologic, hematological, endocrinological diseases, etc.5
Fatigue also progresses in aging, cancer, depression, multiple
sclerosis, Parkinson’s, etc.6 Accelerated life pace and ﬁerce
competitive social lifestyles are making fatigue a commonly
occurring condition. Reports abound of some traditional/
natural medicines and food constituents used as healthcare
products for the relieve of body fatigue include Antrodia
camphorata,2 Radix Rehmanniae Preparata (Shu Dihuang),4
Loach protein,5 dietary nucleotides,7 etc. Additionally, medical
practitioners often advise one to get enough sleep and avoid
staying up late. With these arrays of options for fatigue
alleviation, it is still necessary for patients aﬀected by the
above-mentioned associated diseases to take medication-based
therapies.6 Synthetic drugs or therapies currently available for
fatigue treatment have not been top-notch, and hence, eﬀorts
are directed toward nutritional interventions that are safer and
eﬀective fatigue prevention methods.7 Therefore, it is
imperative to investigate natural antifatigue compounds,8
most of which could improve athletic ability, accelerate the
elimination of fatigue by-products in humans, without
attendant adverse eﬀects.
Natural products have no side eﬀects or cause very few side
eﬀects. They have attracted the attention of many researchers
in the ﬁeld of biomedicine. Many active ingredients have been
used in the development of drugs and functional foods.
Octacosanol (OCT) is a regular monohydric high-grade
saturated linear aliphatic alcohol and an important component
of a natural wax extracted from multiple plants and animals.9,10
It has a diet of interest due to its excellent biological activities;
of interest was the identiﬁcation of OCT as a functional
antifatigue agent, which could enhance endurance and
energy,11 dietary and pharmaceutical supplements,12 and as
feed additives for animals.13 In addition, OCT has an anti-
Parkinsonian eﬀect,14 regulating cardiovascular functions,15
anti-inﬂammatory and antinociceptive eﬀects,16 etc. As a
natural compound, only a small portion gets ingested from
diets. Adding OCT to diet will beneﬁt people’s health.9 In

Nevertheless, most studies about OCT only evaluated its

In our previous work, we found that OCT attenuated

genes and signal pathways regulated by OCT in the muscle
tissues of the mouse over-exercise model. Bioinformatics
indicated the protein−protein interaction network in the
muscle cells after the addition of OCT to the diet. Our study
preliminarily elucidates the antifatigue eﬀect of OCT.
■MATERIALS AND METHODS
Chemicals and Reagents. OCT (purity 99%, ﬂakelike white
powder) was brought from Sigma-Aldrich Company (St. Louis, MO).
Liver glycogen (LG), muscle glycogen (MG), blood lactic acid
(BLA), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), superoxide dismutase (SOD),
and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) assay kits were purchased from
Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). Radio-
immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buﬀer was purchased from
Servicebio (Servicebio Biotechnology Co. Ltd., Wuhan, China, Cat.
G2002). Polyclonal antibody periaxin (Prx) (NBP2-93522), tripartite
motif-containing 63 (Trim63) (AF5366-SP), calcium channel,
voltage-dependent, T type, α 1H subunit (Cacna1h) (NBP1-
22444SS), myosin-binding protein C (Mybpc3) (AB133499), and
glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (no. AT000)
were brought from Cell Signaling Technology Co. Ltd. (Beverly,
MA). Antimouse immunoglobulin G (IgG) horseradish peroxidase
(HRP) conjugate (W4021), and anti-rabbit IgG HRP conjugate
(V7951) were obtained from Promega Company (Madison, WI). All
other chemical reagents of this investigation were of analytical grade.
Experimental Animals and Diet. For the care and treatment of
experimental mice, we followed the guidelines for the ethical
treatment of laboratory animals. We used 7-week-old special
pathogen-free male C57BL/6 mice brought from Hunan SJA
Laboratory Animal (SLAC, Co., Ltd., Changsha, Hunan, China).
All mice were fed with a standard diet in the animal laboratory with
12/12 h reverse light/dark cycles at a stationary temperature of 23 ±
2 °C, relative humidity of 60 ± 4%, and noise less than 48 dB
throughout the experimental period. Before the experiment, mice
were fed with a standard laboratory diet (32% carbohydrates, 22%
proteins, 10% lipids, and 3% vitamins) (SLAC, Co., Ltd., Changsha,
Hunan, China) and tap water. Ethical regulations of Hunan province
on animal experiments and guidelines for the care and use of
laboratory animals were followed in the study. The use of animals and
experimental agreements were approved by the Guidelines for the
Care and Use of Experimental Animals, the Hunan Prima
Pharmaceutical Research Center Co., Ltd. (69181714-X, Liuyang,
Changsha, Hunan, China). The investigation was approved by the
oﬃce of Animal Experiment Ethics at the Central South University of
Forestry and Technology.
Mouse Grouping. The animals were adapted to the new
environment for 1 week before used in the study (Figure 1). During
the ﬁrst week, animals were trained to accustom themselves to swim
twice (5 min per time), and the mice that failed at swimming were
screened out in the period. After that, 30 mice were randomly divided
into three groups (n = 10 per group): control (CON) group (200 mg
distilled water/kg body of weight with no exercise), exercise (EX)
group (200 mg distilled water/kg body of weight with exercise), and
exercise with OCT (EX-OCT) group (200 mg distilled water
suspension of OCT/kg body of weight with exercise).17 We prepared
OCT gavage solution by mixing 50 mg of OCT (purity 99%) to 5 mL
distilled water (contained 1% carboxymethyl cellulose sodium),
oscillated for 5 min by a vortex mixer (MIX-25P, Miou Instrument
Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China), and stored at room temperature.
Animal Operating Methods. All animals received an intragastric
administration using a feeding atraumatic needle once per day at 9:00
am for 30 consecutive days. Their body weight and food intake were
monitored every week. After 30 min of drug administration, a group
of ﬁve mice in the exercise group were exposed to the Sleep
Interruption (KSYY-I 18S418M1, Yikang Technology Co., Ltd.,
Beijing, China) and they were made to perform dynamic movements.
The principle of sleep interruption was applied with the athletics
stimulus. The instrument parameters were is set as follows: speed, 20
s/r; number of turns, 3 r; rest time, 1 min, and the total daily duration
of the experiment is approximately 360 min. The rotation mode is
random (forward or reverse). The mice were forced into constant
motion.
At about 4:00 pm, the mice in the same group were dropped into a
rectangle glass swimming pool (50 × 40 × 30 cm3, freshwater depth
of 25 cm, and maintained at 25 ± 1 °C) for forced swimming test. In
addition, the weights on the tail root of each mouse corresponded to
5% of body weight as evaluated. The swimming time was regarded as
the time spent by the mice ﬂoating in water with struggle and
necessary movements until exhaustion set in. Exhaustion was

This antifatigue screening model with OCT was established
following previous models with slight changes.4 In the last 2 days of
the experiment, the forelimb grip strength, autonomic activities, and
autonomous swimming were completed, and the corresponding
fatigue biochemical index was determined (Figure 1).
Autonomic Activities. Autonomous activity is a common
indicator of an animal’s mobility and is often used to reﬂect the
energy state of mice.21 The tests were performed on the 28th day of
treatment by OCT, after 30 min of intragastric administration, and
the mice were placed in an autonomous chamber in Animal Behavior
Analysis System (JLBehv, Shanghai Jiliang Software Technology Co.,
Ltd., Shanghai, China). After a 5 min adaption time, various behaviors
of the mice were observed, and analysis movement videos that lasted
10 min were recorded; the mice movement tracks, including total
distance (m), average velocity (mm/s), the number of activities
(times), rest time (s), activity time (s), and degree of linearity were
also determined. The test was in triplicates.
Forelimb Grip Strength. The forelimb grip strength of mice was
tested after treatment with OCT supplementation for 28 days. A Grip
tester (YLS-13A, Jinan Yiyan Science and Technology Development
Co., Ltd., Shandong, China) was used to evaluate the forelimb grip
strength of mice.2 The force transducer equipped with a metal rod
(diameter: 2 mm, length: 7.5 cm) was used to gauge the amount of
tensile force for mice. As mice gripped the metal rod, its tail was held
and pulled backward gently while the two forelimbs gripped the rod,
which triggered a “counter pull” causing them to loosen their claws.
This maximum grip force of mice was automatically recorded on a
digital force transducer. The forelimb grip strengths of all mice were
measured on the same day. The test was performed six consecutive
times, and the average measure of maximal force (in g) was recorded;
this low-force system was used to measure the forelimb grip strength
of the mouse forelimb.
Autonomous Swimming Test. Endurance performance is a
signiﬁcant parameter to assess antifatigue, evaluated by autonomous
swimming test. A swimming test was conducted referencing the
previously reported method with minor modiﬁcations.22 On the 30th
day of the experiment, 30 min after the oral administration of OCT,
the mice were taken into a columnar swimming pool (height 35 cm,
radius 10 cm) containing freshwater maintained at 25 ± 1 °C,
approximately 25 cm deep. Mice were placed in the pool and their
behavior recorded over a 3 min test period. The durations of swims
and rests were manually recorded by a trained experimenter during
the last period of the test, and rest was deﬁned when the mice ceased
struggling and were ﬂoating motionlessly on the water, making
minimal eﬀorts necessary to keep their head above the water. The
endurance performance of the mice was surveyed as the swimming
and resting time, recorded from the beginning to exhaustion.
Blood Biochemical Assessments. After 30 days, mice were
euthanized with ethyl ether 60 min after the last intragastric
administration of OCT. After anesthetization, the whole blood
samples were gathered in respective heparinized tubes. The blood
collection was done by removing the left eyeball of each mouse
without anticoagulation. Serum was promptly prepared by centrifu-
gation at 3000 rpm, at 4 °C for 15 min, and then stored at 4 °C till
further analysis. Blood biochemical assessments, blood lactic acid
(BLA), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), superoxide dismutase (SOD),
and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) concentrations, were carried
out using a commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent
assay (ELISA) kit (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Company, Jiangsu,
China).
Glycogen Concentration Analysis. After blood collection, liver
and hind-limb skeletal muscle tissues of mice were immediately
dissected, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and kept at −80 °C until glycogen
concentration analysis was performed as described previously.23 The
brief procedure for glycogen concentration analysis is as follows: 100
mg of liver and muscle tissues were cut, weighed, and homogenized in
the Tris−HCl buﬀer. After centrifugation for 15 min at 13 000 rpm
and 4 °C, the supernatant solutions were used for further experiments.
Tissue extracts (30 μL) or standard glycogen (Sigma-Aldrich Co.,
Ltd., St. Louis, MO) were added to wells of a 96-well plate, and 200
μL of iodine−potassium iodide reagent was added. The 96-well plate
was rested for 10 min before the absorbance value was measured at
460 nm in the ELISA reader. The liver glycogen (LG) and muscle
glycogen (MG) contents were measured using commercial kits
(Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China). All biochemical
parameters were determined using a commercial diagnostic kit
according to the manufacturer’s recommended instructions.
Tissue Sample Preparation. The hind-limb was skinned; the
muscle was separated from the tibia and ﬁbula bones of the
experimental mice. The skeletal muscle and gastrocnemius were
dissected immediately from their surrounding muscles. Muscle tissues
were collected and immediately cleaned with ice-cold saline, stored in
liquid nitrogen, and then kept at −80 °C for genome-wide gene
expression proﬁling analysis. Then, brains, hearts, livers, lungs,
kidneys, and spleens were cut, rinsed in normal phosphate-buﬀered
saline (PBS) solution, and blotted dry. The entire weight and main
organ weights relative to the individual’s body weight (organ
coeﬃcient, %) were recorded and calculated.
Histopathology Analysis. For histological analysis, the midseg-
ments of the gastrocnemius and skeletal muscle tissues were resected,
ﬁxed in 4.0% (v/v) paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h, tissue sections
of mice were made transparent using dimethylbenzene, and ﬁnally,
inlaid in molten paraﬃn. Sections of the gastrocnemius and skeletal
muscle tissues were cut into 5 μm thick slices on a Microtome
(Thermo Histostar), put on clean glass slides, and dried overnight at
37 °C. These sections were stained for 6 min and used for the
[hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained] morphological and
pathological evaluation of muscle cells, inspected under a microscope
equipped with a digital imaging system (BX-43 + MD-50, Olympus
Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).
Gene Expression Proﬁling Analysis. RNA sequencing was
performed at the Shanghai Kangcheng Biological Engineering Co.,
Ltd. (Shanghai, China). RNA quality was assessed using the Agilent
2100 bioanalyzer and the RNA 6000 Nano Chip (Agilent
Technologies, Amstelveen, The Netherlands). RNA samples were
quantiﬁed using the machine of ND-2000 spectrophotometer
(Thermo Fisher Scientiﬁc Company, Waltham, MA). Three gastro-
cnemius RNA samples of mice from each group were stochastically
selected. The SuperScript double-strand complementary DNA (ds-
cDNA) synthesis kit from Invitrogen Company Ltd. was used to
synthesize ds-cDNA from a 5.0 μg total RNA sample. After
precipitation and puriﬁcation with ethanol under 0 °C, 1.0 μg of
ds-cDNA sample was labeled with the NimbleGen One-Color DNA
from the NimbleGen labeling kit (NimbleGen Co., Ltd.) at 37 °C for
2 h under the company’s agreement. Then, 0.1 volume of 0.5 mM
ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) was used to stop the labeled
reaction, and the cDNAs samples were precipitated by isopropanol/
ethanol mixed solution. Gene chips were hybridized at 42 °C with 4.0
μg of labeled cDNA samples in the NimbleGen hybridization buﬀer
plus component A in the hybridization chamber (NimbleGen Co.,
Ltd.) for 20 h. This step was followed by washing in an ozone-free
surrounding, the gene expression chips were scanned by a GenePix
4000B scanner (Axon Co., Ltd.), and signal data of gene chips were
collected. Scanned images of gene chips were imported into the
NimbleScan software (version 2.5) for grid calibration and gene
expression multiple analyses. Expression data of gene chips were
normalized via the quantile normalization method, and the robust
multichip average (RMA) algorithm was calculated by the
NimbleScan. After ﬁnishing the normalization step of raw data, the
probe level ﬁles (*_norm_RMA. pair) and gene-level ﬁles (*_RMA.
calls) were obtained and imported into the Agilent GeneSpring GX
software (version 12.1) for further analysis. Diﬀerentially expressed
genes of gene chips were identiﬁed via fold change (FC) or T-test
ﬁltering between the three groups. The up- and downregulated
expression genes with an absolute FC larger than a selected threshold

Total RNA Extraction, Integrity Test, and cDNA Synthesis.
Gastrocnemius tissue (100 mg/sample) was resected and grounded in
the liquid nitrogen mortar. Total RNA was extracted with the
TransZol-Up reagent from TransGen Biotech Company (TransGen
Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) and the steps were in accordance
with the operation manual of the manufacturer. Extracts were
immediately stored in a −80 °C refrigerator. The quality, purity, and
concentration of total RNA were determined using a Nano-Drop
2000c ultramicro spectrophotometer (Gene Co., Ltd., Beijing,
China). The RNA integrity of samples was checked by a standard
denaturing agarose gel electrophoresis experiment (Bio-Rad Co.,
Ltd.). High-quality RNA samples were chosen for the next step
experiments. About 2.0 μg of total RNA samples were used as a
template to transcribe ﬁrst-strand cDNA synthesis for RT-qPCR
experiments (cDNA reverse transcription kits, Applied Biosystems
Co., Ltd.).
Real-Time Quantitative PCR. Real-time quantitative polymerase
chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was performed using the CFX96 Real
Time-PCR system (SYBR Select Master Mix protocol) (Applied
Biosystems Co., Ltd.) The focus was on peripheral myelin protein 22
(Pmp22), Trim63, unc-51-like kinase 3 (Ulk3), arrestin domain
containing 2 (Arrdc2), Prx, B cell leukemia/lymphoma 3 (Bcl3),
glycoprotein hormones, α subunit (Cga), synaptic vesicle glyco-
protein 2 a (Sv2a), Cacna1h, Mybpc3, and β-actin (control for RT-
qPCR) of antifatigue related genes. The PCR conditions were as
follows: initial denaturation for 3 min at 94 °C; denaturation for 30 s
at 94 °C; annealing for 40 s at 60 °C; extension for 1 min at 72 °C;
and the cycle of 42 times. Gene expression levels of the target genes
were computed based on the 2−ΔΔCt (RQ) formula. The speciﬁcity of
PCRs was analyzed by the dissociation curve and 2.0% agarose gel
electrophoresis (Bio-Rad Co., Ltd.). Primers of genes were designed
using Primer Premier 6.0 software (Premier Ltd., Canada), and are
listed in Table 1. All PCR products were within the linear range of the
exponential phase of PCR ampliﬁcation. Then, the relative expression
levels of target genes were calculated using the Applied Biosystems
software (Applied Biosystems Co., Ltd.), and the expression ratio of
the target gene to that of β-actin is represented in bars.
Total Protein Extraction. Gastrocnemius tissues (100 mg/
sample) were fragmentized in liquid nitrogen precooled mortar; the
homogenized samples were then poured in 400 μL of RIPA buﬀer
(Servicebio Co., Ltd., Cat. no. G2002, Wuhan, China), mixed with 4
μL of cocktail, 4 μL of phosphatase inhibitors, and 4 μL of
phenylmethylsulfonyl ﬂuoride (PMSF) at 4 °C for 1 h. The
suspension solutions were centrifuged at 13 200 rpm for 15 min at
4 °C; then, the supernatant solution was pipetted and protein
concentrations were detected with the bicinchoninic acid (BCA)
protein assay kit (Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai,
China) in accordance with the company’s instructions. The prepared
protein samples (200 μL) were fully mixed with appropriate amounts
of 5× sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) loading buﬀer [10% 2-hydroxy-1-
ethanethiol, 20% glycerol, 4% SDS, 0.3% bromophenol blue, and 125
mM Tris−HCl (pH = 6.8)] heated for 10 min at 95 °C and then
stored at −20 °C.
Western Blot Analysis. About 7−8 μL of protein samples were
denatured by heating and separated through electrophoresis onto a
10% SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) gel, and
transferred to a poly(vinylidene diﬂuoride) (PVDF) membrane.
The membranes hosting the target protein were blocked using 5%
bovine serum albumin (BSA) (2 g of BSA power in 40 mL of 1× Tris-
buﬀered saline with Tween 20 (TBS-T)) at 25 °C for 1 h. After being
blocked, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding
primary antibody (1:1000 dilution in 5% BSA with TBS-T) at 4 °C
for 8−10 h. These were washed thrice (10 min per time) afterward
with TBS-T at 25 °C and re-incubated in a secondary antibody of
antimouse and/or antirabbit IgG HRP (1:5000−10 000) conjugate at
25 °C for 1 h. The membranes were washed thrice (10 min) again
with TBS-T. Immunoreactive proteins on the membranes were
detected using the ECL Plus Western blot detection system (no.
721BR10883, Pierce, Rockford, IL), and the signals of membranes
were recorded in the Gel Imaging system (Chemi Doc XRS+, Bio-Rad
Co., Ltd.). The relative target protein expression compared to the
control protein was determined by calculating the integral optical
density value of each band using the Gel Imaging System.
Statistical Analysis. Each experiment was repeated three times,
and the related data were analyzed by SPSS software (version 2.5,
SPSS, Chicago, IL). Grip results were shown as the mean ± standard
error mean (SEM), and other results were shown as the mean ±
standard deviation (SD) and were analyzed using one-way analysis of
variance (ANOVA) test, followed by Tukey’s test, when comparisons
proceed intergroups and indicated by vertical bars in ﬁgures.
Percentage comparisons were analyzed by utilizing χ2 test. The
probability value p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically
signiﬁcant.
■RESULTS
Eﬀect of OCT on the Body Weight, Food Intake, and
Organ Weight. The body weights, food intake, and main
organs (including brain, heart, lungs, liver, kidneys, and spleen)
of mice involved in 30 days forced exercise experiment were
measured and recorded. In addition to normal growth, there
were no signiﬁcant diﬀerences in the curve of weights and food
intake in the three groups of mice (p > 0.05) (Figure 2A,B).
The changes in the main organ weights were expressed as an
organ/body weight percentage. The results suggest that
continuous OCT supplementation did not inﬂuence the
relative tissue weight when compared with the CON group
(p > 0.05) (Figure 2C).
Eﬀect of OCT on Energy and Endurance Perform-
ance. After 4 weeks of OCT supplementation, the autonomic
activities of mice were analyzed. We observed that the total
distance (p < 0.05), average velocity (p < 0.05), number of
activities (p < 0.01), activity time (p < 0.01), and degree of
linearity (p < 0.05) of the EX-OCT group signiﬁcantly
increased compared to the EX group within the same
recording time (Figure 3), but the rest time (p < 0.05)
decreased (Figure 3D).

Eﬀect of OCT on Glycogen Concentration. Energy
consumption and deﬁciency aﬀect the energy supply and
maximal power output in physical fatigue during exercise.23,25
Stored glycogen in muscle and liver tissues is the primary
source of energy during exercise. In this study, the LG in the
EX group decreased by 11.82% from 63.03 ± 1.79 to 55.58 ±
1.67 ng/mg (p < 0.01) compared with the CON group. OCT
treatment slightly ameliorated exercise-induced LG loss, which
was increased to 58.48 ± 3.81 ng/mg (p < 0.05, Figure 5A).
Moreover, MG followed the same change trend as LG, and the
MG contents of mice in CON, EX, and EX-OCT groups were
347.89 ± 39.29, 315.44 ± 29.77, and 341.65 ± 7.53 ng/mg,
respectively. The MG content in the OCT supplementation
group is increased by 8.31% (p < 0.05, Figure 5B) compared
with the EX group. These results suggest that OCT can slightly
improve the decrease of LG and MG contents of mice induced
by forced exercise and then enhance the exercise endurance of
mice.
Eﬀect of OCT on Biochemical Assessments. Activities
or levels of certain enzymes and biochemical parameters can
forthrightly reﬂect the true status of physical fatigue. They are
widely used to estimate the antifatigue activity of test
substances.26 As shown in Figure 5, exposure to the forced
exercise led to an enhancement in BLA and LDH levels in
comparison to the CON group. The BLA increased the most
after exercise, increased by 11.82% from 3.74 ± 0.88 to 5.33 ±
2.43 mmol/L (p < 0.05). OCT reduced the concentration of
BLA to 4.35 ± 0.90 mmol/L (p > 0.05, Figure 5C), although
the change was not signiﬁcant. Also, the LDH was increased by
18.99% (p < 0.01) after exercise in mice; OCT treatment
observably reduced the LDH content in the blood of mice,
from 19.06 ± 1.52 to 16.03 ± 1.57 ng/mL (p < 0.01, Figure
5D). When compared with the CON group, exposure to the
forced exercise test led to a reduction in SOD and GSH-Px
levels, from 10.12 ± 0.84 and 96.68 ± 12.02 ng/mL to 8.39 ±
0.81 (p < 0.01, Figure 5E) and 84.23 ± 1.80 ng/mL (p < 0.01,
Figure 5F), respectively. Interestingly, OCT inhibited the
above changes, with the SOD and GSH-Px levels increasing to
10.01 ± 0.70 (p < 0.01, Figure 5E) and 95.51 ± 6.55 ng/mL (p
< 0.01, Figure 5F), respectively. Based on these collective
eﬀects of OCT on BLA, LDH, SOD, and GSH-Px, we
conclude that OCT has antifatigue activity in mice, and 200
mg/kg is a potential dose for the development of OCT as an
antifatigue drug and/or functional food. Further study is
warranted to elucidate its antifatigue-related gene regulation.
Eﬀect of OCT on Histopathology of Muscle. Muscle
mass is the main determinant of muscle strength and strength
and is closely related to an organism’s exercise endurance
capacity.27 Histopathological examination of the gastrocnemius
and skeletal muscle did not reveal signiﬁcant lesions between
the CON, EX, and EX-OCT groups (Figure 6). The results
indicated that no clinical signs of tissue-speciﬁc toxicity were
observed after OCT treatments. Nevertheless, all cell nuclei
were situated at the edge of the muscle ﬁbers. In contrast, the
skeletal muscle ﬁbers in the forced exercise mice showed
typical features of striated-muscle degeneration, such as blurred
or dissipated (Figure 6B, as shown in the arrow). Furthermore,
local muscle ﬁber structures became loose, with slight cracks
and twists, and the cell nuclei were more mixed and distributed
in a disorderly manner (Figure 6B, as shown by the arrow).
Likewise, the gastrocnemius muscle of mice also showed these
characteristics (Figure 6A). After treatment with OCT for 30
days, the anomalies were improved to a large extent.

■DISCUSSION
Fatigue can be deﬁned as an exercise-induced inability to
perform an expected or desired work output,24 which could
result in a broad range of physical and mental unﬁtness, such as
inattention, drowsiness, and weakness, and it provokes various
disorders that could be nervous, endocrine, metabolic, or
immune dysfunction, etc.5 Previous studies, including animal
experiments, have conﬁrmed that OCT is able to play an
antifatigue role.11,17,28 The current study demonstrates an
antifatigue activity of OCT in the exercise model and on
several markers for fatigue. Especially, it is shown that the body
weight, food intake, and main organ relative weight were not
markedly changed, and OCT exhibited high safety proﬁles.
OCT supplement could alleviate fatigue symptoms in mice,
caused by forced exercise, which includes enhanced forelimb
grip strength and swimming endurance.11 OCT has a certain
alleviating eﬀect on muscle injury induced by over-exercising in
mice. Under normal circumstances, the myoﬁlament is in a
contraction state and the arrangement was in perfect order and
neat.29 OCT can relieve the slightly blurred or dissipated
muscle ﬁbers caused by forced exercise.
Moreover, the role of OCT in the mice is the attenuation of
the eﬀects of forced exercise induced on the physiological
indicators for fatigue. Previous ﬁndings suggest that recovery
from exercise-induced fatigue requires repair of the injuries
that has already occurred in the body and/or prompts the
elimination of metabolites that accumulate during exercise.8 In
this study, OCT restored the levels of glycogen, BLA, LDH,
SOD, and GSH-Px to baseline. The energy for exercise is
initially generated from the breakdown of MG, and later stages
will be derived from the liver.23,25 LG depletion is an important

B cell leukemia/lymphoma 3 (Bcl3) is a member of the IκB
family and contributes to regulating transcriptional activation
of nuclear factor κ-B (NF-κB) target genes.34 Unaccustomed
strenuous exercise can lead to muscle strength loss,
inﬂammation, and delayed-onset muscle soreness.35 Bcl3 is
an NF-κB transcriptional activator required for muscle atrophy
and binds to the promoters of several genes whose collective
function describes muscle wasting.36 Studies showed that
knockout of the Bcl3 gene in the soleus muscle caused
inhibition of ﬁber atrophy and abolition of NF-κB activity. The
NF-κB and Bcl3 play a critical role in muscle atrophy.37,38
Classical NF-κB signaling is a key factor in controlling
inﬂammation and it has been found that OCT exerts anti-
inﬂammatory properties through the blocking of mitogen-
activated protein kinase (MAPK)−NF-κB/AP-1 signaling
pathway.20 Furthermore, Bcl3 controls Toll-like receptors
(TLRs)-induced MAPK activity.39 Besides, NF-κB can mediate
the protein loss induced by tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)
in diﬀerentiated skeletal muscle myotubes.40 Also Bcl3 directs
the transcription networks of proteolysis and energy
metabolism.36 Our study found that OCT reduces the
expressions of Bcl3 in gastrocnemius (Figure 8B), which
inhibits skeletal muscle atrophy, inﬂammatory transcription
factors, and energy metabolism.
Tripartite motif-containing 63 (Trim63, former MuRF1) is
the direct target of Bcl3, which showed increased Bcl3 binding
to κB sites in muscles.38 In this study, we found an
upregulation of Trim63 expression with OCT treatment
(Figures 8A and 11A). Trim63 mutations were described as
a potential cause for cardiomyopathy and skeletal myopathy.41
The inactivation of MuRF1 can lead to an attenuated muscle
atrophy response.42 Additionally, Trim63 is capable of
polyubiquitination and the ubiquitin-proteasome system
(UPS)-mediated degradation of thick ﬁlament proteins
(Myh6 and Mybpc3).43 Mybpc3 belongs to the immunoglo-
bulin Mybp superfamily and contributes to the maintenance of
muscle structure, regulation of contraction, and relaxation.44
Research studies link truncating mutations in Mybpc3 as the
mechanism associated with cardiomyopathy.45 The missing
Mybpc3 gene leads to a decrease in the myocardial
contractility force.46 Myh6 is also associated with myocardial

■PROSPECT
Fatigue is not a disease in medicine and has not strongly
attracted the attention of medical researchers. In the high-
speed economic society, fatigue almost accompanies our daily
life. Since natural products have no side eﬀects or very few side
eﬀects, it is an important idea to screen antifatigue active
components from natural products. At present, the research on
fatigue and antifatigue in the medical ﬁeld is still at the initial
stage, and the molecular mechanism of fatigue and antifatigue
eﬀects of active ingredients is still unclear. In this study, we
systematically analyzed the eﬀects of dietary OCT on the gene
expression proﬁling of muscle tissue in the over-exercise mice,
identiﬁed the important target genes and related signal
pathways regulated by OCT, and also identiﬁed the network
of protein−protein interactions. The study can provide a
reference for the study of the antifatigue mechanism of other
active components.
